# Compass Hill Estate — Master Property Plan (Southwest Ohio)

**Revision B · September 30, 2026.** Supersedes the "Berm Ring Estate" draft. The estate is now **Compass Hill**. "Berm Ring" stays the name of the Zen garden and storm basin south of the house.

Compass Hill can be built as designed. Checking it against current code turned up five issues, and each one has a fix:

- **NiFe battery bank:** NEC 480.3 requires non-lead-acid batteries to be listed. The bank therefore needs written approval, or a field label from a nationally recognized testing laboratory (NRTL).
- **Drive width:** the S-bend may have to be 26 ft if the fire official measures the dead end as longer than 500 ft.
- **Hose reach:** the south corners of the walkout need either a sprinkler-based extension of the 150-ft hose rule or an emergency lane on the east side.
- **Berm Ring sizing:** it should hold a 6.84-in, 24-hour storm.
- **Budget:** plan with no federal clean-energy tax credits.

**What Revision B adds.** It commits the decisions that were still open (§22.1), lists what is waiting on the land, the energy model, or officials (§17), fills in items that were never specified, corrects the parts of the draft that were missing or wrong, and adds the cart-and-utility tunnel system (§13). Appendix A maps every revision item to the section where it now lives.

## TL;DR

- **Build as designed.** As of September 2026, Ohio still permits houses under the 2019 Residential Code of Ohio (RCO, based on the 2018 IRC). The five-year review is open for comment until Sept. 30, 2026, and it keeps the 2019 RCO as its base. RCO R327 requires UL 9540 listing, and NEC 480.3 requires listing for all non-lead-acid batteries. So the LFP bank goes in during Phase 1, and the NiFe bank waits for written approval or an NRTL field label.
- **Size the Berm Ring for 6.84 in of rain in 24 hours.** NOAA Atlas 14 for Lebanon gives a 100-year, 24-hour storm of 5.70 in (upper 90% bound 6.09 in). The design rule takes the larger of that upper bound and the 5.70-in point value plus 20%, which is 6.84 in. That needs about 11,000 cu ft of storage below the line 12 in under the walkout sills. The ring impounds about 0.25 acre-ft, far below Ohio's 15-acre-ft dam-safety exemption; get the exemption confirmed in writing.
- **Tunnels tie the hilltop together.** A cart-and-utility loop wraps the cistern under the pavilion. Tunnels run from it to the house and the garage now, and to the gate later. Every utility past the meter runs in them. The loop has to be built with the cistern, so it lands in Phase 1.
- **Get the fire-water number early.** Without hydrants, the fire official may size the fire reserve by building volume. My rough estimate is 13,000–32,000 gal, which could take most or all of a 30,000-gal cistern. Ask while you're checking out land, and plan for about 40,000 gal if needed (§3.6).
- **Budget with no tax credits, and phase the work.** The §25D credit ended for expenditures after Dec. 31, 2025. For new construction the IRS counts the expenditure as made when you start using the house, so this house cannot qualify. My own rough placeholder, not a quote, is now about **$19–35M**: $16–28M for the estate plus $3.2–7.3M for the tunnels. House width is the biggest cost dial: each 10 ft adds or removes 1,440 sq ft. The budget ceiling is set after the first professional estimate.

## 1. Executive Summary and Governing Values

A single north–south axis runs from a rural road near Lebanon, Warren County (about 39.4°N), down a south-facing hill. In order along it:

1. A hidden truck lay-by and a gated entrance court.
2. An S-bend drive.
3. A hilltop motor court with a slate-coned pavilion standing on the fire cistern. The cart-and-utility tunnel loop wraps the cistern below.
4. A porte-cochère at the front door.
5. A 120 × 48-ft, three-level ICF house at the brow. It is earth-sheltered on the north and looks south over the "Berm Ring" Zen garden, which doubles as the storm basin.
6. Clean-water outfalls feeding a seasonal pool and meadow.
7. Legacy oak woods in the lower corners, protected by a conservation easement.

The garage and its battery basement sit off the axis, to the west. Tunnels link the house, the court, and the garage below grade now, and the gate later.

**Do these first:**

1. Hire a soils (geotechnical) engineer and a civil engineer now to screen parcels.
2. Hold pre-design meetings with the building and fire officials covering the battery vaults, gate, dry hydrant, fire-water reserve, access length, hose reach, tunnels, and code edition.
3. Once you own a parcel, hire an architect and an energy modeler, and commission the energy model.
4. Get a first professional cost estimate. Then set the budget ceiling and confirm the house width and ICF scope.
5. Set the code-timing strategy (§22).
6. Get insurance quotes on the battery vaults before committing to nickel-iron.

## 2. Parcel, Hill, Search, Due Diligence

**Parcel criteria [F]:**

- **Size:** 25–30 acres. The plan uses about 13; the illustrative parcel is 900 × 1,300 ft, about 27 acres.
- **Slope:** faces south at 8–12%, ideally 8–10% across the 48-ft house depth.
- **Hilltop:** a flat-ish area of at least 250 × 180 ft, plus room for the lay-by.
- **House contour:** about 250 ft wide at the brow.
- **Road:** on the north side, with at least 400 ft of frontage.
- **Downhill land:** owned, for the spreaders, pool, and meadow, and well above creeks and floodplain.
- **Disqualifiers:** springs, seeps, or signs of past slides.

**Where to look [F]:**

- **First choice:** the south-facing north rims of east–west valleys in eastern Warren, Clinton, and southern Greene counties. That means the Little Miami, Todd Fork, and Caesar Creek valleys, near Caesar Creek State Park and Fort Ancient.
- **Next:** Brown, Highland, and Adams counties.
- **Avoid:** flat west-central and northwest Ohio.
- **Avoid:** Cincinnati-area river hills on Kope Formation colluvium (the loose soil that collects on those slopes).
    - ODNR describes the Kope as "a 220-foot-thick, shale-rich unit" and says it slumps and flows when wet.
    - ODNR also cites a USGS finding that Cincinnati has "the highest per capita cost from landslide damage of any city in the United States."
    - A LiDAR-based inventory mapped 842 landslides in the area.
- **Rule:** reject any site with Eden or Fairmount soils, Kope colluvium under the footprint, or slopes over 20% within the building envelope.

**Due diligence [F]:**

| Task | Detail / fix |
|---|---|
| Web Soil Survey + LiDAR | Look for scarps, hummocks, and colluvial aprons |
| County auditor GIS | Property lines, flood zones, CAUV status, easements |
| Site walks | Late winter, and again after heavy rain |
| Geotech borings + slope stability | Do them in the same site visit as the health district's septic soil evaluation |
| Survey | 1-ft contours, which the Berm Ring storage check needs |
| Zoning | Confirm front-yard accessory structures (gate buildings, berms) are allowed |
| Utilities | Public water or a well; sewer or septic type; a natural-gas main along the road or propane; fiber at the road (Starlink is the backup either way). See §17.1 |
| Fire water | Ask the fire official for the required fire-water reserve and whether sprinklers earn a reduction (§3.6) |
| Oak and limestone yield | Have a forester cruise the trees inside the building area (the only oak that can be cut, §14) and have the geotech estimate usable limestone from the borings |
| **CAUV recoupment** | ORC 5713.34 charges "the tax savings on the converted land during the three tax years immediately preceding," recorded as a lien. **Fix:** have the seller pay it or credit it in the purchase agreement, and keep unbuilt acres in qualifying use |
| ⚠ **CAUV and the easement** | CAUV counts woodland only when it is devoted to commercial timber production, and the easement bans harvest. **Fix:** before closing, ask the county auditor which acres can stay in CAUV once eased [A], and have the seller's credit cover every acre that will leave CAUV, not just the building area (§14) |
| Mineral rights / old mines | Check only if searching farther east |
| Bat timing | ODNR/USFWS guidance recommends tree clearing Oct 1–Mar 31 and discourages bedrock work Nov 15–Mar 31 near hibernacula. **Fix:** clear in winter; drill the geothermal bores April–October |

### 3.5 Fire-access checks

- **Turnarounds.** IFC Appendix D, Table D103.4, accepts a 96-ft-diameter cul-de-sac for dead ends of 151–500 ft. The teardrop and the court ring both meet this [F].
- ⚠ **Drive width.** The same table requires 26 ft of width for dead ends of 501–750 ft. The illustrative road-to-court distance is about 520 ft.
    - **Fix [R]:** confirm with the fire official whether Appendix D is enforced and how the length is measured.
    - If it counts as over 500 ft, either widen the S-bend to 26 ft or tighten the bend to bring it under 500 ft.
- **Gate.** Appendix D requires:
    - at least 20 ft of width for a single gate (24 ft is provided);
    - a UL 325 operator;
    - ASTM F2200 construction;
    - approved emergency operation.
- **Load.** Appendix D guidance assumes 75,000-lb fire apparatus. The pavement is designed for 80,000 lb. Tunnel roofs under any fire lane are designed for the same load.
- ⚠ **Hose reach.** The SE and SW corners of the walkout may be more than 150 ft of hose from the court.
    - **Fix [R]:** request the extension the fire code allows for sprinklered buildings, since the house has NFPA 13D sprinklers with extended coverage.
    - **Fallback:** a 20-ft reinforced-turf emergency lane on the east shoulder, kept clear of the septic field.
- **Porte-cochère.** Its 12-ft width is fine because the drop-off loop is not designated as fire access. The 24-ft court ring is.

### 3.6 Fire water: the new catch

Without hydrants, the fire official may size the fire reserve by building volume.

- **My rough estimate [A]:** 13,000–32,000 gal. That comes from the NFPA 1142 method: building volume ÷ 7 (the occupancy factor for a dwelling) × a construction factor of 0.5–1.0. The low end is the 184,000-cu-ft heated box at 0.5; the high end adds the ~40,000-cu-ft attic at 1.0.
- **What it means:** the reserve could take most or all of a 30,000-gal cistern, leaving little for toilets, laundry, irrigation, and the brook.
- **Two things can push it higher:**
    - wood framing above the walkout, which can raise the construction factor to 1.5 (about 39,000–48,000 gal);
    - an exposure multiplier (×1.5), if the official counts another building within 50 ft.

    Keeping ICF on all three floors helps here too.

- **Actions [F]:**
    1. Get the official's number while you're checking out land.
    2. Ask whether the NFPA 13D sprinklers earn a reduction.
    3. Plan for about a **40,000-gal** cistern if the reserve takes most of 30,000.
    4. Keep the fire reserve at the bottom of the tank: everyday pumps draw only from above the reserve line, and only the dry-hydrant line reaches the bottom.

## 5. Motor Court, Pavilion, Cistern, Walk, Porte-Cochère [F]

**Court**

- **Layout:** counterclockwise, 150-ft outer diameter, with a 24-ft two-lane ring that doubles as the fire turnaround.
- **Pads:** seven covered pads on 45° spokes, with the south slot left open for the axis walk. Each has wheel stops and bollard-strength posts. The pads' inner posts stand on the tunnel loop's outer wall.
- **Loop exits:** two exit stairs rise from the tunnel loop between pads on the east and west sides.
- **Walks:** an 8-ft cart-rated walk ring, with raised brick or stone crosswalks.
- **Paving:** exposed local-limestone-aggregate concrete (ODOT freeze-thaw-approved aggregate), domed about 1.5%. **Never use chloride deicers.**

**Pads.** Hipped S1 slate at 8:12 or steeper on white-oak frames with stone or steel post bases. **Build 4 in Phase 1 [F]**, with footings, drains, and conduit for all 7. Their power, including any future EV chargers, comes from the tunnel loop.

**Pavilion**

- **Size and roof:** up to about 50 ft across, with an S1 Vermont unfading green slate cone at 9:12 or steeper, stainless flashing and nails, and stainless snow rails.
- **Frame:** eight site-grown, grader-certified white-oak posts on stone bases, aligned with the spokes, and a laminated-glass lantern at the peak. Site oak goes to this frame first (§7).
- **Finial:** if the lantern carries one, it is bronze-colored stainless steel, not copper (§12).
- **Fireplace:** stone, on the north side. A thickened section of the tunnel loop carries the stone chimney down to firm ground, outside the cistern, and the ash pit empties out from the loop. Stainless flashing and a cricket.
- **Fit-out:**
    - Outdoor kitchen on conduit run in advance; its drain joins the house's pumped sewer line in the loop.
    - Limestone floor.
    - Retractable screens on the west side.
    - Warm dimmable lights.
- **Lightning protection:** aluminum above the roof; copper only where concealed.
- **Drainage:** a stone-lined drip ring piped to the cistern.

**Cistern [F: engineered as the pavilion foundation]**

- **Capacity:** 30,000 gal, with a reserved fire volume at the bottom. Plan for about 40,000 gal if the fire official's reserve takes most of 30,000 (§3.6).
- **Shape:** as the pavilion's foundation it has to reach under all eight posts, so it is roughly **30–40 ft across and shallow**. The earlier 21-ft-wide, 10-ft-deep example was wrong for a foundation tank. Water depths (before freeboard and slabs):

    | Capacity | 30 ft across | 35 ft across | 40 ft across |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 30,000 gal (4,010 cu ft) | 5.7 ft | 4.2 ft | 3.2 ft |
    | 40,000 gal (5,350 cu ft) | 7.6 ft | 5.6 ft | 4.3 ft |

- **Structure:** a thickened roof slab with pier caps under the posts. The tunnel loop wraps tightly around the tank, with a flexible joint between the two structures.
- **Pipes and valves:** every tank pipe passes into the loop, and its valve sits on the loop side. That covers the inlets, outlet, overflow, pump suction, fire-hydrant line, and water-level sensor.
- **Uses:** fire reserve; toilets and laundry; drip irrigation; the brook top-up; the purified line to the NiFe vault. It overflows to the clean outfall.
- **Fire connection:** a fire-approved dry hydrant at the court lane, designed to NFPA 1142 [A: confirm the edition]. Its line leaves the tank through the loop.
- **Access:**
    - Practical entry is a side hatch from the loop into the tank, under confined-space procedures.
    - The ceremonial stone compass-rose hatch on top, aimed at the front door, stays as an inspection hatch. Its inlay is bronze-colored stainless rather than bronze, because it sits in the court's runoff path (§12).
- ⚠ **Settlement risk. Fix:**
    - use a structural tank with waterstops;
    - keep the flexible joint between the tank and the loop, and give every pipe a flexible connection;
    - let the loop, not the tank, carry the chimney and the pads' inner posts.

    Switch to an offset tank if the geotech finds uneven bearing.

**Covered walk.** Gabled slate at 8:12 or steeper, 12 ft wide and 14 ft clear, with an 8-ft path. It has a lit ceiling, and its posts are strong enough to stop a car: bollard-rated steel cores inside the stone or oak cladding [F].

**Porte-cochère.** The one-way drop-off loop passes under a slate porte-cochère at the north door, 12 ft wide and 14 ft clear. It has a lit ceiling and car-stopping posts like the walk. The house cart bay (§13) sits below it.

## 10. Integrated Water Management

**Three streams [F]**

1. **Roof water → cistern.**
    - Passes through leaf screens and first-flush diverters.
    - Stainless leaders run on standoffs or inside hollow stone piers, **never inside timber posts**.
    - The cistern supplies toilets and laundry, gravity-feeds drip irrigation, tops up the brook reservoir, and supplies the NiFe watering system through the purified line. It overflows to the clean outfall; the overflow pipe leaves through the tunnel loop.
2. **Pavement water → planted bioretention creeks.**
    - Oil-absorbent sump inserts at the drains.
    - Trench drains at the court/forecourt seams and along the porch edge.
    - Lay-by and pad runoff goes to swales.
    - Everything ends at the **working outfall**: a 40–60-ft spreader into a filter strip, at least 200 ft from the pool where possible.
3. **Hillside water → routed around everything.**
    - A stone-lined interceptor creek.
    - Step-pool side creeks, with check dams spaced at drop ÷ slope (about 12.5 ft at 12% and 19 ft at 8% for a 1.5-ft drop).
    - Every path ends at the bottom of the property.

Storm drains and creek pipes stay out of the tunnels. They need steady slopes and would flood a tunnel if they clogged, so they keep cleanouts at the surface (§13).

**Berm Ring storage check (planning level)**

- **Geometry [F]:**
    - About 7 ft tall, with a 10-ft crest.
    - 3:1 inner slope and 2:1 outer slope.
    - Keyed compacted fill with a clay core and a toe buttress.
    - Yard about 140 × 70 ft draining to a 140 × 25-ft basin.
- **Design depth:** NOAA Atlas 14 Volume 2, version 3, partial-duration series, at 39.43°N, 84.21°W:

    | Storm | 24-hr depth | 90% confidence interval |
    |---|---|---|
    | 10-yr | 3.96 in | 3.70–4.23 in |
    | 25-yr | 4.63 in | 4.32–4.94 in |
    | 100-yr | 5.70 in | 5.29–6.09 in |

    The larger of 6.09 in and 5.70 × 1.2 governs, so the design depth is **6.84 in [R]**.

- **Volume needed:** about 19,300 sq ft drains into the ring. At 100% runoff with the outlet blocked, that is 19,300 × 6.84 ÷ 12 ≈ **11,000 cu ft**.
- **Volume available:** the basin holds about 8,750 cu ft and the lower-yard wedge about 2,500 cu ft, for roughly 11,250 cu ft total. That barely meets the need.
    - ⚠ **Fix [R]:** make the basin average at least 2.75 ft deep below the storage line, or widen it to 30 ft. The civil engineer re-runs the check with the surveyed dimensions.
- **Outlet:** an unlocked walk-through stone arch (also the emergency exit route) plus an armored spillway. Do a burrow watch every year.
- **Dam safety:** ORC 1521.06 exempts any dam "regardless of height, that has a storage capacity of not more than fifteen acre-feet." The ring holds about 0.25 acre-ft. Get written confirmation from ODNR Dam Safety.
- **Atlas 15 [A]:**
    - NOAA scheduled preliminary estimates for the lower 48 states for peer review by September 2026, with publication in 2027.
    - I found no confirmation that they had been released yet.
    - Re-check the design if the published site depth exceeds 6.84 in.
    - The Warren County Engineer's manual cites the older Bulletin 71 rainfall depths. Use whichever source is more conservative.

**Zen brook [F]**

- Downspouts pour into carved stone basins, which feed a shallow, 6–12-in river-stone brook winding to the basin.
- It reads as a dry streambed between rains.
- **Trickle reservoir [F: include]:** a reservoir at the foot, topped up from the cistern through the tunnel's cistern line, with a solar trickle pump.
- Foundation drains stay piped to the arch, not into the brook.

**Clean outfall [F].** The Berm Ring outlet and cistern overflow go to a 60–100-ft level spreader. From there, water reaches the seasonal pool, which has a low-flow bypass so it dries by mid or late summer, and then spreads as sheet flow across the meadow. **No fish in the seasonal pool, ever [F]:** it stays fishless so amphibians can breed. Build the spreaders and pool **last**.

**No-chloride covenant [F].** Chloride deicers are banned permanently. Use sand or grit; potassium or calcium-magnesium acetate is allowed only on short ramps during ice storms. Record the ban in the house book and in the conservation easement (§14).

**Construction permit (verified)**

- ⚠ **Fix:** put the renewal on the calendar.

**Storm-proofing [F]**

- Every drain has an above-ground overflow path.
- Open roofs are designed to Risk Category III wind.

### 13.2 The loop around the cistern

- **Connections:** the loop meets a chamber on the south side (main tunnel) and one on the north side (gate tunnel). The loop itself works as a turnaround, since carts can just drive around it.
- **Tank pipes:** the loop wraps tightly around the tank, with a flexible joint between the two structures. Every tank pipe passes into the loop, and its valve sits on the loop side:
    - inlets;
    - outlet;
    - overflow;
    - pump suction;
    - fire-hydrant line;
    - water-level sensor.

    A side hatch gives entry into the tank itself.

- **Chimney:** the loop section under the pavilion chimney is built thick enough to carry the stone chimney down to firm ground. The fireplace's ash pit also empties out from the loop.
- **Posts and exits:** the parking pads' inner posts stand on the loop's outer wall. Two exit stairs rise between pads on the east and west sides.

### 13.3 Turnaround spots

- **Chambers:** about 24 × 24 ft each, at six spots:
    - the house cart bay;
    - the forecourt junction;
    - both loop junctions;
    - the garage lobby;
    - the gate end.

    A golf cart can U-turn in one; a utility vehicle needs a three-point turn.

- **House cart bay:** it sits level with the elevator lobby, so there are no steps between cart and elevator.
- **Pull-outs:** side bays halfway along the garage and gate tunnels, for passing and three-point turns.

### 13.4 Cart entrances

- **Garage now:** a covered ramp from the garage loop down to the garage lobby.
- **Gate later:** a second ramp just inside the gate, built with the gate tunnel.
- **Size:** on flat ground, each ramp runs about 90–110 ft at a gentle slope. Where space is tight, a platform lift is the fallback.
- **Weather:** both ramps get roofs and drains at the top and bottom so rain can't run in.

### 13.5 Utilities in the tunnels

Water lines run low on one wall; power and data run high on the other.

- **Power:**
    - Everything past the meter runs in the tunnels: the house service, the garage and its inverters, the gatehouse, the pavilion, court lighting, and the parking pads, including any future EV chargers.
    - Ask the utility to run its own line in its own conduit to a transformer beside the court.
- **Data:** fiber, network, cameras, intercom, gate controls, and the wildlife alert.
- **Water:**
    - Well water enters the garage tunnel through a short sleeve. So do the lines from the buried drinking-water and sprinkler tanks beside the well.
    - Cistern water supplies the toilets and laundry, irrigation, and the brook top-up.
    - A purified line runs to the nickel-iron battery vault.
- **Fire:** the sprinkler main and the fire-hydrant pipe.
- **Sewer:** the house's pumped sewage line to the septic, which leaves the loop on the east side through a short sleeve. The pavilion kitchen drain joins it.
- **Heat (optional):** an insulated hot-water loop from the house's heat tank, to keep the garage basement and gatehouse above freezing.

### 13.6 Serviced from the tunnels

- The cistern valves and hatch.
- The chimney ash pit.
- The garage's inverters and electrical panels, in a room between the lobby and the battery vaults. The vault doors still never open into the tunnel.
- The gatehouse equipment room and the inside doors of the parcel lockers (once the gate tunnel is built).

### 13.7 Stays out, and why

- **Propane:** it's heavier than air and would pool in a tunnel. It stays buried separately along a marked route.
- **Storm drains and creek pipes:** they need steady slopes and would flood the tunnel if they clogged. They keep cleanouts at the surface.
- **Geothermal pipes:** the bore field is south of the house, and its pipes come in through the south wall as planned.

### 13.8 Tunnel basics [R]

These fill in what the tunnel decisions above left open. The structural and civil engineers confirm them.

- **Section:** precast concrete box sections, about 10 ft wide × 9 ft clear. That's one cart lane with room to walk beside it, plus pipes and trays on the walls. Roofs under the court ring and any fire lane carry fire-apparatus loads (§3.5).
- **Waterproofing and drainage:** the same fully adhered membrane and drainage board as the house. Floors slope to sumps at the low points, each with duplex pumps and a high-water alarm to the house panel.
- **Frost:** at least 36 in of cover where the grade allows; insulate the roof near the ramps and exit stairs.
- **Air:** electric carts only, so there's no combustion in the tunnels. Mechanical ventilation, and CO and radon monitors at the house cart bay.
- **Doors:** gasketed, self-closing, fire-rated doors [A] where the tunnels meet the house and the garage lobby. The tunnels stay outside the house's air barrier.
- **Light and exits:** screw-in ceiling fixtures on the house lighting rules, battery-backed emergency lights, and lit exit signs at the stairs and ramps. The two loop stairs, the ramps, the house, and the garage lobby give several ways out.
- **Approvals:** ask the building and fire officials how they classify the tunnels, and whether the house connection needs any separation beyond the rated door [A].

### 13.9 Cost and phasing (my rough estimate)

The loop has to be built at the same time as the cistern, so the first phase now includes:

- the house cart bay and main tunnel;
- the forecourt junction;
- the garage tunnel and its ramp;
- the full loop;
- a sealed stub under the court pointing toward the gate, with the Phase 1 duct bank to the gatehouse (§4).

That comes to about **$2.2–5.2M**, more than the earlier core estimate because the loop, ramp, and chambers all land in that first phase. The gate tunnel, its chamber, and the second ramp come later for about **$1–2.1M**, so the total is roughly **$3.2–7.3M**.
